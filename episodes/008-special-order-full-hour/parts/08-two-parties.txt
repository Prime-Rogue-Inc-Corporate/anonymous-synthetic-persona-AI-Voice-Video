WHITLOW: Madam Speaker, I want to talk about the two parties.
WHITLOW: Every night on television, you see them fighting. Red against blue. Each one tells you the other is the end of the Republic. Each one sends you an email asking for fifteen dollars to stop it.
WHITLOW: And on the things that make television, they fight. They fight very hard. They fight about words. They fight about who said what on which podcast.
WHITLOW: But watch what happens when the bill is about the war. Or the spending. Or the surveillance. Or the bailouts. Watch the vote board.
WHITLOW: The board goes green. Both sides. Big numbers. And the only red lights up there belong to a few cranks like me, from both parties, who get yelled at by both parties for it.
WHITLOW: Here's a secret, Madam Speaker. It's not two parties. It's one party with two gift shops.
WHITLOW: One gift shop sells red hats. One gift shop sells blue mugs. It's the same factory. It's the same donors. It's the same building. They just have different music playing at the register.
WHITLOW: And every two years they ask you which gift shop you want to walk through, and they call that a choice.
WHITLOW: Now, I want to be careful. I'm a member of a party. I'm still a member of my party. I like a lot of the people in it. I also like a lot of people in the other one. That's not the problem.
WHITLOW: The problem is that the job of a party is to win. And the job of a member of Congress is supposed to be to represent people. And in this building, Madam Speaker, the first job eats the second one for breakfast every single day.
WHITLOW: Two weeks ago, the voters threw out the majority. This chamber is about to change hands. A whole lot of new members are coming in January, and every one of them thinks they're going to be different.
WHITLOW: Some of them will be. Most of them, within a year, will discover the meeting. You know the one. The one you have the moment someone hands you a pen.
WHITLOW: I want to say to the new members: welcome. Read the bill. Vote how you said you would. When leadership tells you it's a team vote, ask what the team is.
WHITLOW: And when they tell you you'll lose your committee seats, tell them I said hello. Tell them I lost mine and I'm still here, talking.
