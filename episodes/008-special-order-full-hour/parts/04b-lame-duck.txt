WHITLOW: Madam Speaker, since we're in a lame-duck session, I thought the folks at home might like a little history lesson about lame ducks. I happen to be one, so I consider myself an expert.
WHITLOW: The phrase comes from the old London stock exchange. A lame duck was a trader who couldn't pay his debts. He'd waddle out of the exchange, broke.
WHITLOW: So when you hear Washington call this the lame-duck session, understand that the original meaning was a man who couldn't pay what he owed.
WHITLOW: Which, at forty trillion dollars, describes the whole building. I'm just the only one honest enough to waddle.
WHITLOW: Now, it used to be worse. Until the nineteen-thirties, a new Congress didn't take office until March. So the defeated members got to keep legislating for four whole months after the voters fired them.
WHITLOW: The country decided that was a bad idea. So in nineteen thirty-three, the states ratified the Twentieth Amendment. It moved the start of a new Congress up to January third. Shortened the lame duck to a few weeks.
WHITLOW: That was, I believe, the last time this country made Congress more efficient. It took a constitutional amendment. We should keep that in mind.
WHITLOW: Because even a few weeks is plenty of time for mischief. Think about who's in this building right now.
WHITLOW: Members who lost. Members who retired. Members who are leaving for jobs downtown, with lobbying firms, which they are absolutely not discussing while they're still voting. And members who won, and who are already measuring the drapes.
WHITLOW: None of them answer to the voters anymore. The voters already had their say. That's the whole point of a lame duck. It's the one time of the year a Congress is completely free of accountability.
WHITLOW: Which is why the biggest bills seem to show up in December.
WHITLOW: Think about that, Madam Speaker. The most expensive decisions this government makes, it tends to make in the weeks when the fewest people can be held responsible for them.
WHITLOW: If a business did that, the board would call it a governance failure. Here, we call it the holiday season.
WHITLOW: Now, I'm a lame duck too. I want to be fair. I'm one of the people who no longer answers to the voters.
WHITLOW: So here's what I'm going to do with my freedom. I'm going to use it to tell you what's happening. That's it. That's my whole plan for the lame duck.
WHITLOW: Other members use theirs to pass things. I'm going to use mine to describe them. I think that's the better deal for the taxpayer. It's certainly cheaper.
CHAIR: [gavel] The gentleman will suspend.
WHITLOW: Did I characterize the other body again, Madam Speaker?
CHAIR: No. The Chair wishes to note that the gentleman has said "Madam Speaker" one hundred times.
WHITLOW: Then I'll say it once more for luck. Madam Speaker.
CHAIR: Proceed.
