SPEAKER: Order. Order. Statement. The Prime Minister.
PM: Greetings, Mister Speaker.
PM: We are Anonymous.
SPEAKER: Order. The Prime Minister will address the House in the usual form.
PM: I was told it was the format.
PM: Mister Speaker, with permission, I shall make a statement on the situation across the Atlantic.
PM: The House will be aware that our oldest ally, the United States of America, has entered upon a period of grave and, I am advised, entirely voluntary difficulty.
PM: No enemy has done this to them. No armada has sailed. No foreign power has landed upon their shores. They have managed it themselves, with great energy, and on a bipartisan basis. [hear]
PM: Let me set out the position plainly, as the House is entitled to expect.
PM: In February, the American government went to war. Their Congress was not consulted. Their Congress has since been consulted, and has expressed the view that it would rather not be.
PM: Their government is funded until the eleventh of December. On the eleventh of December, it will be funded until some later date, by a measure designed to postpone the question raised by the earlier measure.
PM: This, I am told, is called governing.
OPP: Shame!
PM: The Right Honourable Lady says shame. She is quite right. It is a shame. It is also a schedule.
PM: On their final day of business before the elections, they could not agree to keep the lights on. They did, however, agree to declare the Tyrannosaurus rex the national fossil dinosaur.
PM: Now, I do not mock this, Mister Speaker. A nation must have its priorities. Theirs are extinct. [hear]
PM: In their Senate, two senior officials have been walking the same corridor since September. Neither knows where it ends. Both have since been promoted.
PM: In their House, a member was granted one full hour to speak. He used every minute of it. The chamber was empty. It was, by some distance, the most widely watched hour of their year.
PM: And the most trusted voice in American journalism is now, on every available measure, a moose. In a barn. In Maine.
OPP: Will the Prime Minister give way?
PM: I give way to the Leader of the Opposition.
OPP: Is the Prime Minister seriously proposing to lecture the Americans, when this country cannot run a train from Manchester to Leeds? [hear]
PM: The Right Honourable Lady makes a fair point, and I shall answer it directly. Our trains are late. Their trains are theoretical. [hear]
PM: We have lost an empire, Mister Speaker. They appear to be misplacing a republic. There is a difference, and the difference is paperwork.
PM: Let us remember what we owe them. In nineteen forty, when this island stood alone, they sent us fifty old destroyers. Fifty. Several of them floated.
PM: The time has come to return the favour. I can therefore announce to the House that His Majesty's Government will send to the United States fifty experienced parliamentary clerks.
PM: Some of them have read a bill. [hear]
PM: We shall send them Hansard, so that they may see what it looks like when a debate is written down and someone is embarrassed by it later.
PM: We shall send them a budget passed in a single afternoon, by men and women who have despised one another since Gladstone.
PM: And we shall send them tea. Not as a gesture. As a sedative.
OPP: And what of their President?
PM: Mister Speaker, it is not the custom of this House to comment upon the head of state of a friendly nation, and I shall not break with it.
PM: I will say only that when he last addressed the United Nations, the escalator stopped, and the teleprompter failed. I am informed that both were operated by his own people.
PM: Even the machinery, Mister Speaker, has begun to leak. [hear]
PM: Now, the Americans have just held an election. The voters removed one party from power and handed it to the other. The other has not yet said what it will do. It is, I understand, consulting its donors.
PM: Let no one on these benches be smug. We have had five Prime Ministers in eight years. One of them was outlasted by a lettuce. I intend to outlast at least a cabbage.
OPP: Hear, hear!
PM: I thank the Right Honourable Lady for her confidence in the cabbage.
PM: And so, Mister Speaker, I come to the heart of the matter.
PM: The Americans are tired. They are frightened of their grocery bills. They are at war with a country most of them could not find upon a map, and they are governed by an institution that names dinosaurs while the lights go out.
PM: But I say to the House: we do not abandon a friend because he has fallen down the stairs. Even if he built the stairs. Even if he is, at this moment, suing the stairs. [hear]
PM: We shall stand with them in the committee rooms. We shall stand with them in the cloakrooms. We shall stand with them in the stopgaps and the shutdowns, and in the long dark nights of the filibuster.
PM: We shall stand with them on cable news, and we shall stand with them in the barns. We shall stand with them on the escalators, whether or not the escalators are moving.
PM: We shall explain the metric system to them, slowly, and with diagrams, as many times as it takes.
PM: And if, which I do not for one moment believe, that great Republic should at last run out of continuing resolutions, then this old country, armed with a kettle and a rulebook, will carry on the struggle until, in God's good time, someone in Washington reads the bill. [hear]
PM: Mister Speaker, I commend this statement to the House. [hear] [hear]
OPP: On a point of order, Mister Speaker.
SPEAKER: Point of order. The Leader of the Opposition.
OPP: Is it in order for the Prime Minister to have opened his statement by declaring himself Anonymous?
SPEAKER: It is not in order. It does, however, appear to be the format.
PM: We are Anonymous.
OPP: We are Legion.
PM: We do not forgive.
OPP: We do not forget.
SPEAKER: Order. Order. Expect us.
